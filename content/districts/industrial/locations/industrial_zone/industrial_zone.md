# Neighborhoods near factories

> Tags: `#landmark` `#industrial_zone` `#working_class`

**Location:** Las Flores City
**Type:** Industrial District

## Overview

When the Chinese mines first opened, workers seeking proximity to the mines and the city started occupying land in this area. As time passed, the influx of immigrants and the expansion of mining operations catalysed the transformation of the landscape from rural to industrial.

---

## Neighborhoods

| Place | Description |
|---|---|
| **Old Settlers’ Nook** | Some of the original houses of the early settlers can still be found scattered throughout the district, many abandoned yet standing as remnants of a bygone era. |
| **New Millennium Row** | This area features modern buildings constructed between 2000 and 2050, showcasing various architectural styles and catering to different income levels. |
| **Factory-Adjacent Residences** | Located near the factories, these residential areas are predominantly inhabited by the working class. As the wealthy moved north, these neighborhoods became central to the blue-collar community of Las Flores. |

## Daily Life

### Daytime

As one navigates the district, the streets are a hive of activity with carpentries, metal works, car repairs, furniture stores, and virtually every type of industry imaginable. Delivery trucks, motorbikes, bicycles, and cars contribute to the bustling atmosphere.

### Nightlife

The zone’s edgier side becomes apparent after dark, with abandoned buildings and green areas occupied by street inhabitants or under the control of local gangs, making certain spots perilous.

---

## Conclusion

The Industrial Zone District remains a testament to Las Flores’ evolution, embodying the city’s spirit of resilience and adaptability. This sprawling district continues to thrive as the industrial heart of the city.
