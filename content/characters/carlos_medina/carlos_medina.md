# Carlos Medina

> Tags: `#figure` `#npc` `#student` `#victim` `#power_systems` `#guild_family` `#alex_group` `#las_estrellas`

## Overview

**Born:** 2054
**Age (2077):** 23
**Role:** Electrical Engineering Student (power systems), building electrical inspector (part-time)
**Status:** Deceased (killed in 2077, in the Week-Four high-speed chase)
**Discipline:** Power distribution and building electrical systems (grid side), the opposite end of the trade from [Carlos Lacan](../carlos_lacan/carlos_lacan.md), who works on devices and electronics at the repair bench.

> **The Carlos of Alex's group.** Carlos Medina is the electrical engineering student Miguel brings into Alex's investigation, and the one killed in the chase. **Carlos Lacan** is a different, living electronics technician from the northern edge who is not part of Alex's investigation (see "Not Carlos Lacan" below). The source story had a single "Carlos"; this file is that character.

## Physical Description
- Hair: Black, unruly, often pushed out of his eyes while working
- Eyes: Dark brown, earnest, hooded, usually focused on a schematic or screen
- Build: Slender, wiry, long face and long neck, lacking physical presence
- Skin: Light brown
- Distinguishing features: Constantly carrying a diagnostic tablet; wears a heavy canvas jacket with the Electricians' Guild patch on the shoulder. In the portrait he wears a grey collared work shirt, open at the neck; the canvas jacket is the lore wardrobe variant.

## Background
Carlos comes from a proud family in the Electricians' Guild district. His grandfather and father were both guild electricians, but Carlos was the first to attend the Universidad Nacional de Las Flores, the public university and engine of social mobility with a respected engineering faculty. He was in his final year of electrical engineering, aiming to work on grid modernization in the neglected outer districts. He had a girlfriend, a job lined up, and a family that was incredibly proud of him.

To pay his way through his final year, Carlos took a side job inspecting building electrical systems. This is how he was assigned to Residencial Las Estrellas.

### Family and trade (expanded; invented details marked)
- **Guild line:** Grandfather and father were guild electricians, so Carlos grew up reading panel schedules and conduit runs before he read novels. He knows the trade as the people who pull the wire, not only as the people who draw it.
- **Where he lives:** In the guild quarter, in the family home, with his parents. His father still keeps the grandfather's tools in a wall cabinet. *(Invented: the cabinet; the household itself is from the existing "proud family" lore.)*
- **Why grid work:** The outer districts lose power on a schedule nobody published. He wants to be the engineer who fixes that, not the one who explains it. This is his stated career aim and the reason the job he had lined up (grid modernization) mattered to him.
- **The family's pride is the pressure:** He is the first at university, so he treats his name on a document as something the whole family signs. This is why he insists on filing everything formally.

## Personality
Carlos was methodical, detail-obsessed, and analytically curious. He wasn't looking for a conspiracy; he simply couldn't *not* notice the anomaly in the Residencial Las Estrellas grid. To him, the deliberate sabotage of the AC systems was an elegant, offensive fault that demanded to be documented. Because he thought in circuits rather than power dynamics, he was occasionally oblivious to social danger, assuming that reporting a fault would lead to it being fixed, not to his own death.

### How it shows (visible behaviors, invented as concrete detail)
- **He photographs before he touches.** Every open panel gets a photo with a pocket ruler laid beside it for scale, then a second photo after. He does this even for a fuse he is about to replace.
- **He corrects labels.** If a breaker is mislabelled he does not mention it, he re-labels it, in block capitals, and logs the change. Colleagues tease him for it; he does not understand why.
- **Quiet, not shy.** He speaks in complete, ordered sentences ("First, the feed. Then the load. Then the part that does not add up") and goes silent while thinking, tablet held flat against his chest.
- **Earnest to a fault.** He assumes a person who has been shown the evidence will act on it. He says "once it is on the record" the way other people say "once it is safe".
- **Not a talker about himself.** Relatives and his girlfriend describe him by what he fixed, not by what he felt.

## The Residencial Las Estrellas Arc

1. **Assignment.** Through his inspection side job, Carlos is sent to Residencial Las Estrellas to check the building's electrical systems. *(Existing.)*
2. **The anomaly.** He finds that the building's AC/HVAC controls do not match the panel schedule: the rooftop unit is fed and switched by something that is not on any drawing. It is deliberate, competent work, and he is offended by it the way a craftsman is offended by a bad weld. *(Existing: the HVAC bypass of the safety protocols and the secondary system that feeds carbon monoxide into the ducts. Mechanics invented.)*
3. **The second finding (invented, optional).** While tracing the feed he also finds that the building's electrical certification was signed off by a contractor who never opened the panels. This is the same family of fraud as the falsified records and owner cover-up in the [Retirement Complex Murders](../../lore/stories/retirement_complex_murders/retirement_complex_murders.md) story, so a formal report would expose the owners, not only the saboteur. It is why fixers, not only a killer, would want him stopped.
4. **Joining Alex's group (Week Three).** Miguel, who knows him from shared coursework, explains Alex's theory about the HVAC murders and the roof apparatus. Carlos says yes before Miguel finishes the sentence (existing story beat), then asks for the request in writing. Within two visits to the rooftop unit he confirms the HVAC system could be hijacked, wires a hidden camera to a passive recorder that can be retrieved without going on the roof, and later supplies the second camera for the vacant room.
5. **The report.** True to his habits he writes it all up in the guild's format, sends a copy to the building administration and a copy to Miguel "in case". It never reaches anyone who acts on it.
6. **The chase (Week Four).** Alex spots the old gasoline car near the building and acts before anyone agrees on a plan. Miguel, Carlos and Ana Kim arrive. The group follows in two vehicles on the road south to the outer districts. A motorcycle cuts across Carlos's lane. It was not random. Carlos, driving one of the cars, dies in the crash; Alex and Miguel abandon the chase, and the motorcycle is never traced. *(Existing.)*
7. **Aftermath.** His death is the central wound of the investigation, the moment it stops being abstract. Alex never stops carrying it; Miguel becomes more careful with others' lives; Ana Kim's decision to leave is partly shaped by it. His family is informed by the guild before the news does. *(Last point invented.)*

## Role in the Story
Carlos is the investigation's first loss. He found the AC sabotage before the group did, joined Alex's group through Miguel, confirmed the HVAC theory, installed the cameras and died in the Week-Four chase. His death is what turns the group's amateur inquiry into a personal reckoning. His demise is meant to feel like a real loss: a bright, careful future extinguished by corporate greed.

## Game Design Notes
- **Archetype:** The Honest Inspector. Competent, correct, and out of his depth in a world that does not run on correctness.
- **Dialogue style:** Ordered, precise, short sentences; never jokes; gives figures and sources. Contrast with the separate Carlos Lacan, who jokes and narrates.
- **Likely uses:** Memorial and recollection content for Alex's group; a recovered copy of his report as a clue; the tablet as an item.
- **Expressions needed:** neutral, focused, surprised, afraid, determined (all present in the prompt file).
