## Overview

Yifan Wang is a Beijing-born International Relations student at **Universidad del Valle** and president of the **UDV Chinese Students Association**. He treats campus as a foreign posting: build soft power, defend Chinese cultural dignity, network upward, go home with a career. In 2077 he is twenty-two — charismatic, impatient with “both-sides” framing of Chinese capital in Las Flores, and already collecting allies and enemies in equal measure.

## Background

Raised on national pride and institutional ambition. Chose UDV for prestige and for the laboratory of a city shaped by Chinese lithium money — he wants to understand Las Flores the way a future diplomat understands a host country: interests, narratives, who to invite to dinner.

Within his first year he won the CSA presidency and turned a quiet club into workshops, networking nights, and cultural showcases. His signature push: a **National Day** celebration of Chinese music, dance, and cuisine meant to make Chinese presence impossible to treat as invisible or purely extractive.

## Campus Politics

- Challenges administrators when Chinese students feel sidelined
- Sharp tongue; long-game patience
- Loyal circle of CSA organizers; vocal critics among students who see him as an LW-adjacent nationalist
- Views lithium politics as complicated: he will not accept China-as-villain caricature, but he is not (yet) Yi Sang — he still argues in open forums

## Dragon Phoenix Orbit

Faction tag `dragon_phoenix` places him in the Chinese commercial–community gravity well around figures like **Wang Mei Li**’s trading world: guest lists, sponsorship soft asks, “have you met…” introductions. Treat as **orbit**, not employment — unless a future plot hires him.

## Daily Life

East Asia international dorm: political theory stacks, Mandarin news feeds, CSA run-of-show notes. Small framed photo of Tiananmen Square on the desk — home as destiny, not souvenir. Late nights arguing in group chats and booking venue deposits.

## Goals

Near-term: nail National Day; grow CSA influence; land internships with embassies / trade bodies / Dragon Phoenix–adjacent firms.
Long-term: Chinese foreign service or a major IO — policy at scale. UDV is the launchpad.

## Personality & Mannerisms

- Personality: `ambitious_nationalist`
- Charismatic in rooms; sharper in rebuttal than in small talk
- Humor exists, usually pointed
- Flaw: conflates critique of LW crimes with anti-Chinese sentiment — can shut down allies who are actually after the cover-up, not the diaspora

## Player Interaction Dynamics

### The Meet
Campus quad, CSA event setup, library debate corner, or a Dragon Phoenix–sponsored student mixer. He clocks whether you are press, activist, classmate, or recruiter in three sentences.

### Opening Posture
- Friendly, performative hospitality
- Will pitch the association and correct “misconceptions” about Chinese students / investment
- Tests if you flatten China into Minera Estrella

### Softening Conditions
- Player separates diaspora students from corporate crimes
- Helps with National Day / venue / security / media
- Offers real IR career access or honest debate without racism
- Shares intel that protects Chinese students during protests (not LW executives)

### Payoff
- Campus access, CSA volunteers, cultural-event cover
- Introductions into young Chinese professional networks
- Argumentative ally who can open Northeast / Chinatown doors — or argue the player’s case in student media

### Risk
- If the player paints all Chinese actors as the leak, he becomes a public critic and organizer against them
- Can rally students to shout down a vigil or an exposé panel

### Gameplay Role
Campus political node — soft power, nationalism, youth pipeline into Chinese institutional futures. Foil/ally tension with anti-mining activists (e.g. María Gutiérrez’s register) and LW security (Yi Sang’s world he does not fully see).
