# Isabella Quispe

**Title:** High School Student "Chabela", San Pedro de los Pescadores

> Known affectionately as **Chabela** to her family, friends, and everyone in the village.

**Physical Description:**

- Warm copper-brown skin deepened by years of relentless high sun over the Far South river valley.
- Broad, strong cheekbones and a nose with character; deep, steady brown eyes that have learned the river's moods.
- Long straight dark hair often wind-tousled from the Río de las Flores breezes, sometimes pulled back with a simple practical tie or ribbon.
- Sturdy, capable build from helping haul nets and carry catches; callused hands, sun lines at the corners of her eyes.
- Practical clothes suited to a small riverside town and school: faded blouse or simple school shirt, dark skirt or sturdy trousers, river sandals dusty from dirt paths. Often wears a small woven bracelet or a river-smoothed stone on a cord.

**Description (full):**

Isabella "Chabela" Quispe is a 17-year-old high school student in San Pedro de los Pescadores who has made the deliberate, almost rebellious choice to stay. Everyone in the village calls her Chabela.

While her classmates talk endlessly about moving to Las Flores for "real opportunities," Isabella has decided the opposite. The river is her home. The brutal midday sun that bleaches the adobe and bakes the docks is part of her. After what happened to her aunt, leaving feels like betrayal.

Her aunt left when Isabella was eight, chasing work and a different life in the big city. At first there were letters, then visits that grew rarer and more strained. Eventually the news came in fragments: bad jobs, the wrong people, a cheap room far from any river. When they learned she had died alone, something in the family cracked. For Isabella, it crystallized into a vow: *I will not leave this place to die somewhere else. I will make San Pedro strong enough that staying is not a failure.*

She helps her family on the boats when school allows, knows which bends of the Río de las Flores are generous and which are dangerous, and quietly gathers other young people who also feel the pull to remain. They mend nets, document the old stories before the elders are gone, watch the water for changes the mining upriver might bring, and dream of a future where a good life does not require abandoning the village.

Chabela breaks the most common stereotype of her generation not with anger, but with stubborn, sun-baked affection for home.

## Background

- Born and raised in San Pedro de los Pescadores along the banks of the Río de las Flores.
- Family has fished and tended small riverbank plots for generations.
- Everyone in the village has called her **Chabela** since she was small.
- The departure and death of her aunt became the defining wound of her childhood and the reason she rejects the "everyone leaves" narrative.
- Attends the modest local high school; many of her friends already talk about transferring to schools in Old Las Flores or the city as soon as they can.

## Personality & Traits

- Quietly stubborn and deeply loyal to place.
- Observant about the river, weather, and people — the kind of attention that comes from growing up where survival depends on noticing small changes.
- Warm with those she trusts, reserved or politely firm with outsiders who pity "poor remote villages."
- Carries grief for her aunt as fuel rather than paralysis.
- Finds joy in small acts of care: mending a net properly, teaching a younger child to read the water, making sure the old stories are told.

## Daily Life

- Walks or bikes the dusty paths between home, school, and the docks.
- After school helps unload boats, sort catch, or work the small family garden.
- Spends evenings on the riverbank or plaza listening to elders, sometimes writing down what they say.
- School uniform is practical and often a little worn; she doesn't try to look like a city girl.

## Goals

- Make it possible for people her age to build real lives in San Pedro without having to leave.
- Protect the river and the fishing that sustains the town from upstream damage.
- Keep the community's memory and pride alive so the next generation doesn't see leaving as the only path to dignity.

## Fears

- The slow emptying of the village as more young people disappear into the city.
- The river changing (less fish, more pollution, altered flow) until the old way of life is impossible.
- Being seen as naive or "stuck" for choosing to stay.
- Repeating her aunt's isolation, but in reverse — watching everyone she loves leave until she is the one left behind in an empty town.

## Quote

Chabela: "The city took my aunt. It will not take me."
