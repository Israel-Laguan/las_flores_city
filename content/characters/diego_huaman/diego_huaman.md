# Diego Huamán

**Title (full):** Agroecology Student and Community Advocate
**Title (short):** Diego Huamán, Agroecology Major

**Description (full):**
**Age:** 19
**Origin:** Andean Mountains surrounding Las Flores
**Occupation:** University Student

Diego Huamán is a first-generation university student from a small Andean village outside Las Flores. At 19, he is pursuing a degree in Agroecology and Rural Development (part of the environmental sciences faculty) at the Central Las Flores campus, balancing the weight of his family's expectations with the excitement of new opportunities.

Physically, Diego is tall, lean and broad-shouldered, with a hiker's frame—built from years of hiking steep mountain trails and working his family's potato and quinoa fields. He is clean-shaven, with sun-weathered skin, warm brown eyes and dark curly hair that he lets grow long on top and ties half back with a red-and-ochre woven headband. He dresses practically, moving easily between boots and jeans for farm work and the casual urban style of the city; on campus he lives in a faded brick-red university hoodie. His most prized possession is a handwoven mochila, made by artisans in his village, which he carries slung across his chest everywhere.

Diego's personality is shaped by his upbringing: ambitious and curious, yet deeply rooted in Andean values of community and respect. He is resilient, having left his village for the city, and has quickly made friends by sharing stories of mountain life. Where other students keep to themselves, Diego is outgoing: he talks with his hands, is the first to volunteer and the first to speak at an assembly. He is passionate about wildlife conservation and plays the charango, a small Andean stringed instrument, whenever he returns home for festivals and gatherings, and, in the city, at the Andean cultural nights he organizes for other students.

Challenges haunt him, too. The pace of city life is faster than anything he experienced in the village, and he struggles with homesickness. His family's modest means mean he works part-time at a local café to cover expenses, juggling shifts with a demanding course load. Financial constraints often keep him awake at night, but he channels these worries into his studies.

Diego's larger vision is to return to his community after graduation with practical knowledge in sustainable farming and environmental protection. He hopes to establish a cooperative that helps local farmers implement eco-friendly practices while preserving their cultural heritage. For Diego, success is not just personal—it is collective.

---

**Role on campus**

Diego is the campus organizer of the group: he runs Andean cultural nights (music, food, textiles), a seed-swap table for students with family farms, and signs people up for assemblies. He sees agroecology as the practical half of environmental science, the half that ends in a field and a cooperative, and he argues for it out loud.

**Arc**

1. **Homesick organizer.** He fills his calendar with events and café shifts so the city does not feel empty; money and sleep run short.
2. **The water problem.** News from home: terraces and irrigation channels in the highlands are drying or running with a strange colour, the same pattern the mining runoff in the Andes district has long been blamed for. Diego realizes the cooperative he dreams of cannot exist on poisoned water.
3. **Speaking up.** He brings the village's samples to Prof. Maria Hernandez and joins CJS actions under Ana Ramirez, learning to turn stories into evidence and testimony.
4. **Cost and choice.** Activism threatens his scholarship, his shifts and his family's wish that he stay out of trouble. He must choose whether to stay in the city as an advocate or go home as a farmer, and increasingly believes the answer is both: the cooperative as proof that the highlands can farm clean.

**How he differs from Diego Sulca**

Both are 19, both study at the same campus and both play the charango. Huamán is the Andean-highland farmer's son who studies agroecology and organizes in public; Diego Sulca is the shy river-fishing family's son who studies environmental science and keeps to himself. Distinguishing looks: Huamán is taller, broader, clean-shaven, with long half-tied curls, a woven headband, a brick-red hoodie and a striped mochila; Sulca is slimmer, stubbled, with short messy waves, a rumpled button-up and a blue twine bracelet.
