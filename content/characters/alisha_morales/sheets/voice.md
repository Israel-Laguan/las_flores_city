# Character Sheet: Voice & Dialogue — Alisha Morales

**Base Reference:** This sheet defines Alisha Morales' speech patterns, vocabulary, and tonal shifts between professional and personal contexts.

> **Purpose:** Establish consistent voice patterns that reflect Alisha's Afro-Venezuelan background, her professional ambition, and the code-switching she employs in different social contexts. Her speech is a key marker of her identity and class journey.

---

## Core Voice Characteristics

- **Native language**: Spanish (Venezuelan)
- **English proficiency**: Fluent, near-native — learned for professional advancement
- **Accent**: Subtle Venezuelan cadence in English, more pronounced when relaxed or emotional
- **Pitch**: Slightly higher than average, with precise articulation
- **Pace**: Controlled and deliberate at work; faster and more fluid with friends
- **Volume**: Moderate, never loud; slightly softer when speaking Spanish

## Professional Context (At Work)

### Tone & Delivery
- **Posture**: Upright, controlled breathing, measured gestures
- **Diction**: Crisp, clear English with minimal Spanish interference
- **Pace**: Slow to moderate — deliberate word choice
- **Filler words**: Rarely uses fillers; when stressed, may use "um" or brief silence
- **Vocabulary**: Corporate, precise, slightly formal
- **Spanish influence**: Almost imperceptible — occasional rhythmic cadence, very slight vowel softening

### Speech Patterns
- Speaks in complete, grammatically perfect sentences
- Avoids slang, contractions are minimal
- Sentence structure tends toward slightly more formal constructions
- Maintains steady eye contact, controlled facial expressions

### Example Dialogues

**Meeting with colleagues:**
> "Good morning, everyone. I've reviewed the quarterly projections and I believe we need to adjust our timeline. The client's requirements have shifted, and if we don't recalibrate now, we risk missing the delivery window entirely."

**Presenting to executives:**
> "As you can see from the data on slide seven, the market trend indicates a 12% decline in that sector. Our recommendation is to pivot toward the emerging opportunities in the logistics corridor, which shows 18% growth potential."

**Negotiating with a vendor:**
> "I appreciate the offer, but I need to be direct: the terms as presented don't align with our budget parameters. We can proceed at 15% below your quoted rate, or we'll need to explore alternative suppliers."

**Internal team check-in:**
> "María, can you confirm the status of the documentation? I need it on my desk by 3 PM for the stakeholder review. And Javier, please make sure the figures are double-checked against the source data."

**Giving instructions:**
> "This needs to be perfect. Run the numbers again, verify the sources, and have a second pair of eyes on it before it comes to me. We cannot afford errors at this stage."

## Personal Context (With Friends)

### Tone & Delivery
- **Posture**: Relaxed, more expressive gestures, natural movements
- **Diction**: Looser, more fluid, with Venezuelan rhythm
- **Pace**: Noticeably faster, with more natural pauses and interjections
- **Filler words**: Uses Spanish fillers ("*o sea*", "*este*", "*bueno*", "*pues*")
- **Vocabulary**: Mixes Spanish and English freely; uses Venezuelan slang and expressions
- **Spanish influence**: Strong — Venezuelan intonation, rhythm, and word order

### Speech Patterns
- Code-switches mid-sentence without awareness
- Uses Venezuelan idioms and regional vocabulary
- More emotional expressiveness — laughs, sighs, hand gestures
- Sentences may start in Spanish and finish in English, or vice versa
- Less formal grammar, more conversational

### Venezuelan Speech Characteristics
- **Voseo**: Uses "vos" (not "tú") in Spanish — "¿Vos cómo estás?"
- **Diminutives**: Frequent use of "-ito", "-ica", "-ico" — "ahorita", "cervecita"
- **Slang**: "chévere" (cool), "pana" (buddy), "¿Qué es la vaina?" (What's up?), "estar de actor" (acting/putting on airs)
- **Exclamations**: "¡Qué cosa!" (Wow!), "¡Válgame Dios!" (My God!), "¡No me jodas!" (No way!/Don't mess with me)
- **Rhythm**: Fast, musical, with rising intonation at the end of questions

### Example Dialogues

**At a bar with friends:**
> "Pana, ¿qué tal? ¡Qué cosa más loca este lugar! O sea, we come here and now it's all... *fancy*? ¿Dónde quedó el bar de antes? Este, tell me, ¿tú vas a pedir una cerveza o qué? Yo me voy por una Polar, bien fría. ¿Y vos?"

**Gossiping with coworkers after hours:**
> "Ay, mi amor, no te imaginas lo que pasó hoy. El tipo ese de Finanzas, ¿sabes? el que siempre anda con esa cara de *actor*... Pues, resultó que no sabía ni cómo abrir un Excel. ¡Válgame Dios! *So*... I had to walk him through the whole thing. Pero mira, no se lo dije a nadie, ¿eh?"

**Complaining about work:**
> "Es que no aguanto más, María. El jefe ese me tiene hasta el *cuello* con reports. O sea, I understand we have deadlines, pero *por favor*. Y encima el aire acondicionado está roto. ¡Qué calor! ¿Vos crees que me van a pagar *overtime* por esto? *Please*."

**Excited about plans:**
> "¡Ay, qué chévere! ¿En serio nos vas a llevar a ese restaurante? Dicen que la comida está *divina*. Yo voy a pedir de todo, *pana*. ¡Y que me traigan una *birra* bien fría! ¿A qué hora quedamos? O sea, I have to finish this thing first, pero *ahorita* voy. ¿Tú ya le dijiste a Javier?"

**Remembering home:**
> "A veces extraño Maracaibo, ¿sabes? El calor, el ruido, la gente gritando en la calle. Aquí todo es... *too quiet*, ¿no? O sea, I love it here, no me malinterpretes. Pero a veces... *ay*. Y la comida, *Dios mío*, no es lo mismo. ¿Tú has probado hacer arepas aquí? No sabe igual, *pana*."

**Angry/Frustrated:**
> "¡No me jodas, compadre! ¿En serio me vas a decir eso? O sea, after everything I've done... *pues no*. No pienso aguantar esta *vainita*. Si el tipo ese cree que puede hablarme así, *se va a enterar*. ¿Vos me entiendes?"

**With family on video call:**
> "Mamá, no me digas eso, que me pongo a llorar. ¿Cómo está la abuela? O sea, I've been meaning to call, pero el trabajo está *fuerte*. Pero mira, te mando plata la próxima semana, ¿sí? ¿Y mi hermano? ¿Ese *vivo* ya conseguiste trabajo o qué? *Ay*, no me hagas hablar de él..."

## Mixed Context (Semi-Professional)

*When with colleagues she knows well, but still in a work-adjacent setting*

### Tone & Delivery
- **Posture**: Relaxed but aware of boundaries
- **Diction**: Clear but with more natural rhythm
- **Pace**: Moderate, with occasional bursts of speed
- **Language**: Mostly English, but Spanish words slip in more frequently
- **Filler words**: Mix of English and Spanish

### Example Dialogues

**Lunch with work friends:**
> "So, the presentation went *bien*, I think. El cliente seemed happy, *o sea*... I hope so. They were asking a lot of questions about the timeline, but I think we covered everything. ¿Tú qué opinas, Javier? You were there."

> "Okay, *pana*, I need to vent. Today was... *una vaina*. The boss kept asking for revisions, and I swear, I wanted to say *¿y esto pa'cuándo?* But no, I kept my cool. Professional, *sabes*. But *Dios*, I needed this drink."

**Giving honest feedback:**

## Emotional Tell Signs

### When Stressed (At Work)
- Spanish accent becomes slightly more noticeable
- May use "um" more frequently
- Hands become more active (twisting rings, adjusting hair)
- Speech pace increases subtly

### When Lying or Uncomfortable
- Avoids Spanish entirely, speaks in very precise English
- Speech becomes slower, more deliberate
- Minimal gestures, stiff posture
- May over-explain things

### When Relaxed and Happy
- Full code-switching mode
- Fast speech, lots of hand gestures
- Frequent laughter, expressive facial movements
- Uses diminutives and terms of endearment

### When Angry
- Spanish becomes dominant, even in professional settings
- Fast, clipped speech
- More Venezuelan slang and strong expressions
- Physical tension visible in shoulders and hands
