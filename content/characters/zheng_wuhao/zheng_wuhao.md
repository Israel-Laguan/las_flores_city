## Physical Description (2077)

- Hair: Thin white and steel-gray, kept short out of old habit
- Eyes: Dark brown, still tired and guarded; the wariness has settled into the bones of his face
- Build: Once stocky and solid; age has softened the shoulders and slowed the walk, but he still plants his feet like a man used to concrete floors and shift change
- Skin: Fair, deeply weathered; liver spots on the backs of work-worn hands
- Distinguishing features: Knuckles mapped with old scrapes; a cheap sport earbud still clipped to one earlobe (habit more than music); thermos instead of clipboard; practical clothes that look like retirement versions of workwear; safety-boot gait even in soft shoes

**Portrait note:** Existing assets (`zheng_wuhao__default.png` and expression variants) show him in his early 50s — the disaster-era likeness around 2052–2055. Treat those as historical / case-file imagery unless a dedicated elderly 2077 portrait is authored.

## Overview

Zheng Wuhao was Facility Supervisor at Minera Estrella's container facility — the first company man on site the night the Great Lithium Leak began. He treated a catastrophe like a containment problem, downplayed it up the chain, and years later repeated the rainfall story under oath. By 2077 he is seventy-seven, twenty years past an early retirement he accepted during the company's post-leak modernization purge. Most neighbors know only "old Zheng who used to work the mine." The player can find the rest — if they bring patience and proof.

## Background

Zheng came up through Minera Estrella's Chinese technical pipeline: competent, procedure-obsessed, conflict-averse. He was good at keeping a 24/7 facility moving — shift handoffs, pipe inspections, incident forms that made problems look manageable. That skill is also why the company trusted him, and why the cover-up found him useful.

He was never invited into the rooms where Li Wei's people chose narratives. His power was middle-management power: what got written down first, and how urgent it sounded.

## Night of the Leak — August 15, 2052 (~age 52)

On a reduced-staff night, a corroded high-pressure pipe carrying lithium hydroxide failed at the container facility ~10 km from the Andean Mine. Zheng was first to respond. He opened containment and cleanup under the assumption the leak could be handled internally. His initial report to the Vice President of Minera Estrella soft-pedaled the scale.

Psychology of that night (for writers):
- Fear of escalation and looking incompetent
- Habit: facilities survive by absorbing problems before they become scandals
- Incomplete information in darkness / reduced staffing — and a refusal to admit how incomplete
- Loyalty reflex: protect the company, protect his people, protect his job

By morning, dead fish and river discoloration made the lie of "manageable" impossible. An executive dispatched by the VP saw the true damage. The report climbed toward the LW Group. Zheng's understatement had already bought the conspiracy its first delay.

## Cover-Up and Trial

Zheng was not in the August 19, 2052 secret meeting that approved the staged electrical-failure narrative. Leave that door slightly ajar for player probing — he may have suspected fabrication later; he may have chosen not to look.

What is established:
- He became a **defense witness** in Sessions 6–7 (February 15–22, 2055), attributing the failure to **heavy rainfall**
- Camera logs from that night show **no rain** on external facility cameras (see vault clue: Security Footage Metadata — Aug 15, 2052)
- The broader cover-up — bribed technicians, staged explosion, silenced witnesses — was run above his pay grade by LW Group operators (Zhang Liang, Elena Torres's media line, Qiu Bo's technical staging, et al.)

At trial he was ~55: still useful as a sober supervisor who could say "weather" with a straight face. Whether he was coached, threatened, or sincerely clinging to a story that let him sleep is a live question the player can pressure.

## Retirement (~2056, ~age 56)

After the 2055–2057 trial era and Minera Estrella's modernization push (AI processes, workforce cuts, early retirement / transfer offers), Zheng took the exit. Officially: age and restructuring. Unofficially: a man who had become a liability and a reminder.

He left the facility orbit, stopped answering journalists, and practiced being nobody.

## Life in 2077 (~age 77)

Zheng lives modestly on the **industrial fringe** of Las Flores — cheap rent, few questions, shift-worker neighbors who keep their own hours. Days are small: thermos coffee, a folded newspaper or tablet news he pretends not to skim for Minera Estrella names, walks that never quite reach the river.

He is cut off from LW Group power but not free of its shadow. He still flinches at strangers who ask precise questions. Guilt sits quieter than fear most days; both wake when the Río de las Flores is in the headlines.

Health: ordinary old age more than contamination — he was upstream at the facility, not in the poisoned downstream barrios. The poison he carries is procedural.

## Personality & Mannerisms

- Speaks in procedures: times, forms, "as reported," "per protocol"
- Deflects moral questions into technical detail
- Polite until cornered; then thin, tired steel
- Rare flashes of grief when talk turns to workers on shift or families downstream
- Still half-defends the rainfall story aloud — not because it is true, but because the alternative collapses twenty-five years of self-mythology
- Personality tag: `conflicted_supervisor` → weary witness

## Player Interaction Dynamics

### The Meet
The player finds him in a low-stakes everyday setting — park bench near the industrial fringe, a cheap café, or the hallway outside a small apartment — **not** at Minera Estrella. He looks like someone's grandfather who still dresses for a job that ended two decades ago.

### Opening Posture
- Polite refusal
- Company-line weather story
- "That was a lifetime ago."
- Claims he was just doing his job; implies the player should leave ghosts alone

### Softening Conditions
Trust rises when the player brings proof or respect instead of a prosecutor's opening:
- **Internal Safety Report — Container Facility (2048)**
- **Security Footage Metadata — Aug 15, 2052** (no rain)
- Liu Fang / Cecilia Pérez materials that prove pre-leak knowledge
- Tone: listening, not threatening; protecting him from spectacle

### Payoff
Partial confession, not a full corporate organigram:
- Admits he **downplayed the first report**
- Admits the **rainfall testimony was incomplete / coached / convenient**
- Can interpret old shift logs, pipe inspection habits, and who would have signed what
- Will **not** casually name Li Wei-level conspirators unless trust is high and the player has already built the upper case elsewhere

### Risk
If the player is loud, corporate-aligned, or threatening:
- Zheng shuts down
- May tip someone that "people are asking again"
- Becomes unavailable or actively hostile as a witness

### Gameplay Role
Living witness / testimony unlock for the Great Lithium Leak mission and mystery overlay. Optional quiet ally for reading facility procedure and old industrial paperwork. Never a boss fight; never the sole cause of the disaster.

## Dialogue Tree Hooks

- Duty vs. truth
- Middle-management culpability ("I filed the report" as both confession and excuse)
- Memory vs. official narrative
- Fear of dying with the lie intact
- What early retirement really bought — silence, not peace
- The difference between "I didn't know" and "I didn't want to know"

## Scene Placement

- **Location:** Industrial fringe café, park bench, or modest apartment threshold (South / industrial edge of Las Flores)
- **Available time:** Daytime or early evening; avoid making him a night-mission spectacle
- **Mood:** Guarded → brittle → exhausted honesty if cracked

## Story Arc Potential

### Early (Lithium investigation)
Rumors and old personnel lists point to a retired supervisor. First meeting yields weather and doors closing.

### Mid
With vault clues in hand, the player returns. Zheng confirms the understated first report and the rotten rainfall testimony — enough to reopen motive and timeline.

### Late
If protected and respected, he becomes a quiet corroborating voice: procedures, names of mid-level signatories, how containment "should" have worked. If burned, he vanishes into silence or warning calls.

## Notes for Writers

- Keep him human: no cartoon redemption arc, no cartoon villainy
- He is a link in the chain, not the architect of the cover-up
- Bilingual flavor is fine if consistent with other Chinese LW / Minera staff; do not stereotype
- Portrait assets remain early-50s until an elderly variant exists — call that out in UI/case files if needed
- Do not kill him off to tidy the theme; his living testimony is the point
