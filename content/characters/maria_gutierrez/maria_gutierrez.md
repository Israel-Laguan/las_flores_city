## Physical Description

- Hair: Vivid dyed color — electric violet / magenta with darker roots, cut in a sharp asymmetrical lob or undercut sides; never “newsroom neat”
- Eyes: Dark brown, fierce and unblinking; left brow often arched higher
- Build: Lean, wiry, moves like someone who has walked picket lines and overnight stakeouts
- Skin: Warm medium-brown Latina complexion; small scar on right brow from fieldwork
- Distinguishing features: Multiple piercings (septum, eyebrow, helix / cartilage stacks, occasional lip stud); ink peeking at collarbone or knuckles; thrifted leather / patched denim over practical black layers; press badge worn like a dare; small sport earbud clipped in; notebook or battered phone always in hand

## Overview

María Gutiérrez is an investigative journalist at **El Informador** who writes like a molotov in print. Her breakout piece — *"Mayor Hernandez's Legacy: A City Choking on its Own Greed"* — tied environmental degradation, corporate capture, and mayoral patronage into one readable indictment. In 2077 she is still in the fight: lithium residue in the water, LW Group soft power, and a city that keeps selling its lungs for export contracts. She is not neutral. She is a leftist who uses the byline as a weapon and the street as a second newsroom.

## Background

Raised in a working-class barrio where the Río’s yellow-green seasons were family lore, María learned early that “objectivity” often meant protecting the powerful. She studied journalism on scholarships, cut her teeth covering labor actions and neighborhood assemblies, and landed at El Informador as the paper that would still print stories La Prensa’s advertisers might choke on.

She keeps one foot in the newsroom and one in the movement — union meetings, mutual-aid kitchens, student occupations — without pretending the two never talk. Sources trust her because she shows up after the cameras leave.

## Signature Work

### "Mayor Hernandez's Legacy: A City Choking on its Own Greed"
Long-form investigation for El Informador exposing:
- Environmental degradation downstream of industrial corridors
- Corporate influence over permits and enforcement
- How Mayor Hernandez’s “development” language laundered extraction and displacement

The piece made her hated in city hall and quoted in barrio WhatsApp groups. It did not end the machine. It named it.

## Politics & Personality

- Openly leftist: redistribution, environmental justice, anti-extractivism, distrust of LW Group philanthropy
- Fierce, impatient with both-sides framing
- Loyal to sources; contemptuous of access journalism
- Humor is dry and cutting; warmth reserved for comrades and the people she writes for
- Flaw: can burn bridges with editors and allies who want “softer” framing; treats caution as collaboration

## Player Interaction Dynamics

### The Meet
Player finds her outside a rally, in a cramped El Informador side office, or at a late-night café rewriting copy with headphones in. She sizes the player up in one glance — comrade, tourist, or cop-adjacent.

### Opening Posture
- Sharp, testing questions
- Will not hand over files to someone who smells like PR
- Offers a cigarette or a URL before she offers trust

### Softening Conditions
- Player brings documents, photos, or testimony that advance a real story (lithium, permits, Hernandez-era leftovers, LW Group)
- Player protects a source or shows up for a community action
- Player argues politics honestly instead of performing neutrality

### Payoff
- Tips, unpublished notes, introductions to organizers
- Co-byline pressure / public amplification of the player’s findings
- Optional ally path in media / investigation arcs

### Risk
- If the player leaks her sources, sides with corporate narratives, or treats her as aesthetic flavor, she cuts contact and may burn the player in print

### Gameplay Role
Information broker with a moral compass pointed hard left. Quest-giver for investigation and activism threads. Not a romance-first character unless the player earns ideological and personal trust over time.

## Dialogue Tree Hooks

- Objectivity vs. solidarity
- What “development” costs the south
- Hernandez’s long shadow vs. new faces with the same pockets
- Whether exposés change anything without organizing
- Burnout, rage, and staying sharp without becoming bitter

Player reads her Hernandez piece or meets her covering a spill / permit fight. She is useful and prickly.

### Late

## Notes for Writers

- Keep the colored hair, piercings, and fierce look consistent — this is her present-day identity, not a costume
- She is Latina; avoid East Asian / Northern European facial defaults in art
- Do not soften her into “quirky reporter”; her politics are load-bearing
- El Informador ≠ La Prensa (Lina Kim) — different paper, rawer register
- Age in 2077 is **~32** (born ~2045)
