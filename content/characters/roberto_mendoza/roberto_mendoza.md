# Roberto Mendoza

**Title:** Retired Minera Estrella Process Engineer ("Don Beto")

**Physical Description:**
- Latino man in his 70s with a lean, wiry build, weathered tan skin, sunken dark eyes, thin white receding hair swept back and deep worry lines. A stiff hip makes him walk slowly with a wooden cane; he wears thin-rimmed spectacles only to read papers.

**Description (full):**

The elder of the two Mendoza brothers. Roberto was a process and piping engineer at Minera Estrella: he looked after the high-pressure lines that carried liquid lithium hydroxide between tanks, signed their inspection sheets, and was the man whose signature sat at the bottom of the corrosion reports. When the 2052 leak happened, the line that failed was one he had asked, in writing, to replace. He was not among the building owners at Residencial Las Estrellas, but his knowledge of the pipe systems and containment failures helped prove the cover-up in 2077. He lived in fear of retaliation for decades, and only came forward when it became clear the movement had the strength to protect him.

**Age (2077):** 72 (b. 2005)
**District:** Las Flores (Old Las Flores)
**Role:** Retired Minera Estrella Process Engineer
**Descendancy:** Latin American

## Career

- Studied engineering at Universidad Nacional de Las Flores and joined Minera Estrella in the early 2030s, as the plant was being commissioned (invented).
- Process and piping lead under Facility Supervisor Zheng Wuhao: pressure lines, valves, corrosion tracking, shift-handoff inspection sheets.
- In 2047-2048 the auditor Cecilia Perez flagged the corroded sections of his lines; his written request for replacement was buried and he was pressured to countersign a deferred-remediation waiver instead (invented detail; fits Cecilia's suppressed audit reports).
- Declined the 2062 offer to join the Residencial Las Estrellas buy-in, which is why he was not a building owner (invented reason; the "not an owner" fact is existing).

## Why he stayed silent

Two things kept him quiet for twenty-five years: the waiver with his signature on it, and his son. His son, Javier Mendoza, was a young safety inspector at Minera Estrella in 2052 and later retracted his trial statement after a payoff. Roberto knew that exposing the cover-up meant exposing Javier (invented family link, see below). He came forward only after his brother Carlos had done so first and the movement could protect both.

## What he gave the 2077 investigators

The pressure-line logs, the valve and corrosion records, and the waiver itself. These show the breach was a mechanical corrosion failure, not an electrical fault, which cuts the legs out from under the staged "electrical failure" story used to scapegoat Luz del Rio.

## In 2077

- Lives in Old Las Flores, alone, with a cane and a kitchen table full of folded schematics. Neighbours call him "Don Beto" (invented nickname).
- Cautious, measured, slow to speak; answers questions with questions and wants things in writing.
- Visible traits: cane, stiff gait, thin white hair swept back, tan skin, spectacles only for reading. No hand tremor (that is his brother's).
